name: pick-the-remedy-that-keeps-the-exemplar
description: When a finding offers "fix the instance or soften the rule", check whether the rule CITES that instance as its exemplar — softening then costs the rule its worked example
metadata:
  type: project
---

A mismatch between a stated absolute and one site that violates it
comes with two offered remedies, and they are not symmetric when the
rule quotes the violating site *as its own illustrative example*.
CLAUDE.md's sdlc sweep paragraph says every consumption-point mention
of a principle is a bare pointer, and then sells that with two named
exemplars — one of which ("When a teammate escalates") was the site
restating the rule. Softening the absolute would have left the
paragraph asserting a weaker rule *and* citing, as its model of
compliance, a site that no longer models it. Trimming the site's
paraphrase repaired both ends at once.

**How to apply:** before picking between "fix the instance" and
"soften the claim", grep the claim's own paragraph for the instance's
name. A hit means the instance is load-bearing for the rule's
exposition, and the instance is the end to move. Then re-read the
absolute for a *scope* defect the finding did not name — the same
paragraph's "every other mention" turned out to also sweep an
introductory naming that was never a consumption point, repaired by
restricting the scope rather than by weakening the "never restated"
verb. Related:
[[pick-the-fix-that-spares-the-expensive-verification]] is the same
choose-the-cheaper-end judgment where the cost is a proof rather than
an example, and [[new-hedge-obsoletes-the-old-absolute]] is what
happens when you soften anyway.
